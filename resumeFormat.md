For your specific goal—getting hired fast as a dual SWE/NE student—you must use the **Hybrid (Combination) Resume Format**. 

Do not use a purely Chronological format (which highlights your work history—bad for a student) or a purely Functional format (which highlights skills but hides where you learned them—looks suspicious to recruiters). 

The Hybrid format puts your **Skills and Proof (Projects) above your Work History**. It says: *"Here is exactly what I can do, here is the proof, and oh, by the way, here is where I did it."*

Here is the exact, battle-tested section order you should use, optimized for the "extreme stand-out" strategy:

---

### The Exact Section Order

#### 1. The Header (Top 1 inch)
*Keep this incredibly clean. No photos, no flashy colors.*
*   **Left Side:** Name (Large, bold), Phone Number, Professional Email, Location (City, State).
*   **Right Side:** Links to your GitHub, LinkedIn, and **your QR Code**. (Make the QR code about 1x1 inch so it doesn't waste space, but is instantly visible).

#### 2. Professional Summary (3-4 lines max)
*This is your hook. No fluff.*
*   **Format:** 1 sentence defining your dual-niche (NetDevOps/SRE). 1 sentence highlighting your top tech stack. 1 sentence stating your career goal or top achievement.
*   *Example:* "CS Graduate specializing in the intersection of Software Engineering and Network Architecture (NetDevOps). Proficient in building scalable applications (Python, Go, C++) and automating the infrastructure beneath them (Terraform, Ansible, Cisco IOS). Seeking to leverage homelab-to-production experience to build resilient cloud networks."

#### 3. Technical Skills (The Grid System)
*Put this third so the ATS (Applicant Tracking System) instantly scores you a 100% match, and the human recruiter instantly sees you check their boxes.*
*   **Format:** **Do not use paragraphs or commas.** Use a 2-column or 3-column table/grid format with bold categories.
    *   **Languages:** Python, C++, Go, Bash
    *   **SWE & DevOps:** Git, GitHub Actions, Docker, REST APIs, CI/CD, Microservices
    *   **Network & Cloud:** AWS (VPC/EC2), Cisco IOS, TCP/IP, OSPF/BGP, Wireshark
    *   **IaC & Automation:** Terraform, Ansible, YAML, Python Netmiko

#### 4. Key Projects (The Meat of the Resume - Put this ABOVE Experience)
*This is the most important section. Since you are a student, your projects are worth 10x more than your part-time jobs.*
*   **Format:** 
    *   **Project Name** (Bold) | *Technologies Used: [Tech 1, Tech 2]* | [Month, Year]
    *   **[Insert tiny Architecture Diagram here, aligned right]**
    *   2-3 bullet points starting with strong action verbs. Bold your **metrics and results**.
*   *Ordering:* Put your most impressive SWE/NE hybrid project first. Follow with your best pure SWE project, then your best pure NE project. (Limit to 3-4 projects).

#### 5. Professional Experience (Including Homelab)
*If you have real internships, great. If not, your Homelab goes here.*
*   **Format:** Job Title | Company/Homelab | Dates
*   **Ordering:** Reverse chronological. 
*   *Crucial Rule:* Even if you worked at a coffee shop or did IT helpdesk, write the bullets to lean toward your career goal. 
    *   *IT Helpdesk example:* "Automated routine network troubleshooting using custom Python scripts, reducing ticket resolution time by 15%."
    *   *Homelab example:* "Independent NetDevOps Engineer | Self-Employed (Homelab). Architected a segmented home network using pfSense VLANs and deployed a Kubernetes cluster via Ansible to host self-developed web applications."

#### 6. Certifications (The Trust Signal)
*Because you are a student, industry certs carry massive weight. Put this above Education.*
*   **Format:** Certification Name (Abbreviation) | Issuing Organization | Month, Year Obtained
*   *Example:* Cisco Certified Network Associate (CCNA) | Cisco | Expected May 2024

#### 7. Education (The Baseline)
*Keep this short and at the bottom. Everyone has a degree; not everyone has a CCNA and a custom Load Balancer project.*
*   **Format:** Bachelor of Science in Computer Science | University Name | Graduation Month, Year
*   *Optional:* You can list your GPA *only* if it is above a 3.5. If it's lower, leave it off and let your Projects and Certs do the talking. Do not list relevant coursework unless it is highly specific and not obvious (e.g., "Advanced BGP Routing" is worth listing; "Intro to Java" is not).

### Critical Layout Rules for the "Extreme" Strategy

1.  **The "Above the Fold" Rule:** In journalism, the most important news is "above the fold" of the newspaper. On a resume, it's the top 1/3rd of the page. When a recruiter opens your PDF, their screen should show **your Header, Summary, Skill Grid, and the start of your best Project (including the diagram/QR code)** without them having to scroll. 
2.  **One Page Only:** As a student, you must keep it to one page. If you have too much, cut your weakest project or trim fat from your experience bullets.
3.  **ATS Visual Balance:** Because you are using a QR code and suggesting an architecture diagram, you must ensure the rest of the document is heavily text-based and cleanly structured. Do not use wild colors, weird fonts, or heavy graphical elements elsewhere, or the ATS robot will scan a blank page and auto-reject you. Use standard fonts (Calibri, Arial, Roboto, Garamond) and standard black text with perhaps one dark blue or dark gray accent color for headers. 
4.  **Save as PDF:** Never submit a Word doc (.docx). Word docs can scramble your two-column skill grid and ruin your diagram placement. Always "Save As" or "Export to" PDF.
