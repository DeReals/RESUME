## Questions you're likely to get

**Background & motivation**
- *"Walk me through your background — what led you to apply here?"* Present → past → future: what you're doing now (Loyola CS, Technology Assistant, Ledger, club leadership) → what first pulled you toward CS/coding (fill this in honestly) → why this role specifically appeals to you now.
- *"Why Code Super Powers? What do you know about us?"* Point to their mission of expanding STEM access to communities without easy access to instructors, their games-first teaching approach, and that they specifically recruit Maryland college grads.
- *"Why do you want to work with kids?"* Anchor it in something real — the moment a club member, or someone you helped in IT support, finally "got it" is a fine starting point if you don't have a sharper answer yet.

**Skills & experience**
- *"What languages/tools are you comfortable with? Have you used Scratch, Roblox, or Minecraft modding?"* Lead with Python/JS and Ledger, then handle the gap directly (see above).
- *"Tell me about a time you explained something technical to someone non-technical."* Pull a specific instance from Technology Assistant or Carroll County IT support — specific beats general.
- *"Tell me about a time you led a team or a project."* Your hackathon story — see the STAR template below.

**Situational (how you'd handle kids)**
- *"A student's frustrated because their project keeps breaking — what do you do?"* Normalize the frustration briefly, don't fix it for them, ask what they've already tried, break the fix into one small next step, let them drive.
- *"Two kids are arguing over a computer or whose turn it is."* Separate calmly, restate the expectation neutrally, redirect energy back to the project fast rather than litigating who was right.
- *"How do you keep a room of K-8 kids with different skill levels engaged at once?"* Pair faster kids as informal helpers, keep a stretch task ready for early finishers, check in individually instead of only teaching to the front of the room.
- Even in a background-focused interview, it's worth having one concept ready to explain "as if to a student" in plain language — pick something from Ledger or the pothole-detection project and practice explaining it in one or two simple sentences.

**Logistics & fit**
- *"What's your availability, and can you travel to sites in PG County, Anne Arundel, and DC?"* Know your actual weekly hours before you walk in — vague answers here read as a real red flag for a part-time/contract role. Bowie HQ is roughly 45–60 minutes from Loyola depending on traffic, so factor that in for any in-person commitment.
- *"We're outcome-oriented and results-focused — how do you take feedback?"* Use a real example of feedback you acted on — a cert-study correction, a code review comment — not just the claim "I'm open to feedback."
- **Possible icebreaker**: given how hard they lean into the "superpowers" branding, don't be caught off guard by "what's your (coding) superpower?" Have something specific ready — not "I'm a fast learner," but something like "I can usually spot the one line breaking a build" or "I'm good at making an intimidating topic feel approachable," tied to a real moment.

## Rehearse this one out loud: your hackathon story (STAR)

- **Situation**: what prompted the hackathon, your role in organizing it
- **Task**: what you were actually responsible for — turnout, logistics, sponsors/judges, day-of operations
- **Action**: 2–3 specific things you did — what you coordinated, what broke, what you fixed live
- **Result**: turnout, feedback, what you'd change next time — end on outcome, not just effort

Fill in the real specifics before noon. A concrete story with real numbers beats a polished-sounding vague one every time.

## Smart questions to ask them

- What does a typical week look like for an instructor — session length, group size, and how much lesson-planning support is built in around the self-paced curriculum?
- What does training look like before I'd run a session independently?
- How are site assignments made — do instructors get a say between Bowie HQ, other PG County/Anne Arundel/DC sites, and remote sessions?
- What does "outcome-oriented" actually look like in practice for instructors — how is success measured?
- Is there a path from this internship into other roles at CSP for people who stick around?
